UltraIterator

Android app · in testing iPhone soon

Fractal artwork by Dan Wills, in an audio-responsive journey.

Your browser should be drawing the fractal background image live from the fractal's Ultra Fractal parameters, with no pictures downloaded. Drag to look around; pinch, or Ctrl and scroll, to zoom.

About two thousand fractals, discovered over many years, drift past as we zoom into the details, moving in response to ambient audio or music.

What it does

  • Motion

    Pictures drift, turn and dive into their own zooms. A whole chain of renders, each a close-up inside the last, plays as one continuous dive.

  • Sound

    It listens to what's playing, through the microphone or the phone's own audio, and moves with it. You can send any part of the sound to any control.

  • Light

    Colour grades, a halo lamp and glints, all worked out on the phone's GPU.

  • Strangeness

    Warps, a Cave and a Corridor to fly through, relief that turns a picture into a landscape, and a vine growing through it all.

  • Your touch

    Drag to steer and hold to pause. A menu of pebbles and pearls tunes everything, and your settings save as presets.

Live fractals

Each picture in the app is a render about two thousand pixels across, and zooming past it would normally show nothing more. More than 2,500 of the fractals have also been ported from Ultra Fractal to GPU shaders, and each port has been checked against its render. In the app this is still in testing: pinch past one of those pictures and the phone carries on drawing the fractal itself, as deep as the hardware can hold out.

The fractal at the top of this page is one of them, drawn the same way by your browser.
