UltraIterator

Ultra Fractal artwork by Dan Wills, on your phone, moving to music.

Your browser is drawing this live from the fractal's Ultra Fractal parameters, with no pictures downloaded. Drag to look around; pinch, or Ctrl and scroll, to zoom.

About two thousand fractals, made over many years, drift past and dive into each other's zooms, in time with whatever's playing around you.

What it does

  • Motion

    Pictures drift, turn and dive into their own zooms. A whole chain of renders, each a close-up inside the last, plays as one continuous dive.

  • Sound

    It listens to what's playing, through the microphone or the phone's own audio, and moves with it. You can send any part of the sound to any control.

  • Light

    Colour grades, a halo lamp and glints, all worked out on the phone's GPU.

  • Strangeness

    Warps, a Cave and a Corridor to fly through, relief that turns a picture into a landscape, and a vine growing through it all.

  • Your touch

    Drag to steer and hold to pause. A menu of pebbles and pearls tunes everything, and your settings save as presets.

Live fractals

Each picture in the app is a render about two thousand pixels across, and zooming past it would normally show nothing more. But more than 2,500 of the fractals have been ported from Ultra Fractal to GPU shaders, and each port has been checked against its render. So past the render, the phone keeps drawing the fractal itself, as deep as the hardware can hold out.

The fractal at the top of this page is one of them, and your own browser is (hopefully) drawing it the same way.
